Quantum Dynamics and Information

How can an isolated quantum system develop an effective arrow of time when its microscopic evolution remains unitary and reversible?

This finite circuit demonstrates information spreading and reversibility. It is not, by itself, a test of the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis.

What the experiment establishes

Thermal-looking local behaviour is compatible with exact microscopic reversibility.

Local loss of memorySimple observables forget the initial perturbation

The reduced states become harder to distinguish even though the complete states remain perfectly distinguishable.

Information spreadingThe missing information resides in correlations

It is no longer attached to a particular qubit. Recovering it requires increasingly nonlocal measurements.

ReversibilityThe inverse circuit reconstructs the initial state

Applying the gates in reverse restores local memory. The apparent arrow of time is therefore an emergent, observational statement.

The HoloQUBITS question

How can this mechanism be tested beyond equilibrium?

The proposal studies quenches holographically and asks whether ETH-type descriptions continue to hold during black-hole formation. One planned diagnostic compares two independently defined critical length scales.
